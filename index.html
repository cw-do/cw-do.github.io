I am an instrument scientist at the Spallation Neutron Source and the Point of Contact for EQ-SANS, its time-of-flight small-angle neutron scattering beamline. My current work is on the instrument itself: machine-learning methods that get more out of every neutron, software that makes the beamline easier for its users, and LLM agents that run the workflow from experiment to fitted model. I came to this from soft matter — polymers, polyelectrolytes, biomolecular assemblies — which is still what the instrument is for.

Current focus

LLM agents and autonomous workflows

Large language models are changing what a beamline can do for its users. ESAC put an LLM assistant on EQ-SANS, answering instrument questions and driving experiment automation from natural language. SasAgent goes further: a multi-agent system that takes small-angle scattering data from ingestion through model selection to a fitted result without a human in the loop. EQSANSCLI wraps the reduction pipeline in an interactive, agent-ready command line. The goal is an experiment that plans, runs, and interprets itself, with the scientist deciding rather than operating.

  • SasAgent: Multi-Agent Artificial Intelligence System for Small-Angle Scattering Data AnalysisJ. Appl. Crystallogr. 2026
  • ESAC (EQ-SANS Assisting Chatbot): Application of Large Language Models and Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Enhanced User Experience at EQ-SANSSoftwareX 2025

Machine learning and statistics for scattering data

Neutrons are scarce, so the question is how much information each one carries. Kolmogorov–Arnold networks invert scattering profiles directly to structure. Bayesian Gaussian-process regression desmears USANS data without the instabilities of classical deconvolution. Optimal binning of correlated measurements and variational allocation of counting time decide, before and during an experiment, where the beam time is best spent. binningverdict turns one of those decisions into a tool users can run.

The instrument and software for its users

Since 2017 I have owned EQ-SANS end to end — data acquisition, sample environment, and the reduction software the user program depends on. I co-developed drtsans, the reduction toolkit now standard across ORNL's SANS instruments; characterized and corrected the inelastic-scattering and detector-geometry artifacts specific to a time-of-flight SANS instrument; and co-designed EXPANSE, a wide-angle spin-echo spectrometer for the Second Target Station. The chopper system is currently moving from four disks to six.

Soft matter foundations

Polymers, polyelectrolytes, and ion transport

How do polymer chains arrange themselves in solution, in melts, and around ions? Neutron scattering with contrast variation answers this in a way few other probes can. This thread runs from the Li⁺ transport mechanism in PEO electrolytes — neutron scattering, dielectric spectroscopy, and molecular dynamics in one study — through the conformation of dendrimers, bottlebrushes, and star polymers, to the chain shape of conjugated polymers in solution and the self-assembly of carbon nanotubes in polymeric systems.

Sustainable plastics

As Co-Investigator on a DOE polymer-upcycling project led by Tomonori Saito (Chemical Sciences Division, ORNL), I use SANS and molecular dynamics to follow how plastic waste is taken apart at the molecular level: the organocatalytic deconstruction of mixed plastics, and the non-monotonic swelling of PET chains in HFIP/DCM solvent mixtures. Both made journal covers.

  • Solubilization of PET in Binary Mixture of HFIP and DCMPhys. Chem. Chem. Phys. 2026
  • Selective Deconstruction of Mixed Plastics by a Tailored OrganocatalystMater. Horizons 2023

Biological and hybrid soft materials

Proteins, lipid membranes, and polymer–protein hybrids studied under native conditions. Recent work with the Tezcan group showed how synthetic polymer chains coat ferritin cages inside polymer-integrated protein crystals, letting the lattice expand reversibly by up to 80%. Earlier: the elasticity of lipid vesicles under attack by pore-forming peptides, hemostatic two-dimensional nanosilicates, and hysteresis-free nanoparticle-reinforced hydrogels.
